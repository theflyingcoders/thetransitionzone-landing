## The short version

|  | Fiberglass | Carbon fiber |
|---|---|---|
| Power | Usually livelier, more pop | Often more muted, but many newer carbon paddles are built for power |
| Control | Good, a bit more forgiving | Usually more precise |
| Spin | Moderate | Often higher, thanks to surface texture |
| Price | Usually lower | Usually higher |

These are tendencies. A carbon paddle with a thin core or a thermoformed build (face and edges moulded as one piece with heat) can be very lively, and a fiberglass paddle with a thick core can be very controlled.

## Fiberglass

Fiberglass faces flex a little more, which gives a springier response. That is popular with players who want power without swinging hard, and with beginners who want a forgiving paddle at a lower price.

## Carbon fiber

Carbon fiber is stiffer. Stiff faces tend to feel more precise on touch shots and give you a clearer sense of where the ball landed on the face. Many carbon paddles also have a gritty, textured surface that bites the ball, which is where much of the spin comes from. You will see labels like 3K, 12K and 18K, which give the number of carbon filaments in each strand of the weave, and terms like raw carbon and T700, which describe the finish and the grade of fiber. None of them tells you on its own how a paddle will play, so compare how it plays, not the label.

## Surface texture wears

The texture that gives you spin is the first thing to go. Many players report spin dropping before any other change in how a paddle feels. See [how long pickleball paddles last]({{< relref "/guides/how-long-do-pickleball-paddles-last" >}}). If you play sanctioned events, remember that USA Pickleball sets limits on surface roughness for approved paddles, so check the current approved list before you buy a very gritty paddle.

## Cores matter too

| Core | Usually feels like |
|---|---|
| Polypropylene honeycomb | Soft, quiet, controlled. The most common core today. |
| Nomex | Hard, loud, powerful. Less common now. |
| Aluminum | Light and soft, with less power. |
| Foam | Used in some newer paddles. The feel depends a lot on the design. |

## Which should you buy?

- You want a forgiving, lively paddle on a budget: fiberglass.
- You play doubles, want touch and spin, and accept the higher price: carbon fiber.
- Not sure: pair the face with a core thickness that matches your game. Read the [thickness guide]({{< relref "/guides/pickleball-paddle-thickness" >}}) next.
